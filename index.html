Phones Without Accounts

Insight brief and methods note. The survey excluded Amhara, Tigray, Somali and Harari (about 30% of the population).

One in five adults in Ethiopia's 2024 Findex survey owns a mobile phone but has no account, and most of those phones can't run an app.

My part: weighted 1,001 Findex responses in PostgreSQL and matched the published 48.8% account rate before going further, then designed the brief: a waffle for the headline, this dot plot, and ranked reasons.

Africa Digital Finance Indicators

Interactive indicator dashboard, 35 countries

58% of account holders in Ethiopia's sample made no digital payment in a year, the highest point estimate of 35 African countries. The 35-country average is 13%.

My part: defined nine indicators with a written rule and base for each, computed them for 35 countries with a population-weighted 35-country average, and designed the scorecard, ranked chart and indicator dictionary.

How this work was made

I loaded the survey data into PostgreSQL and checked the weighting against published figures before any analysis. Ethiopia's weighted figures match the World Bank's published values (48.8% account ownership, 9.5% mobile money, 20.7% digital payments).

I worked with an AI assistant (Claude) on the analysis plan, queries, chart code and copy, and reran every figure in SQL myself. One example of a check: my effective-sample-size calculation gives a design effect of 1.69 for Ethiopia, which matches the World Bank's published figure, so the uncertainty ranges rest on the right sample size.

Review log

  1. 15 Oct 2026, portfolio reviewer

    Checked survey coverage and found that the Ethiopia sample excluded Amhara, Tigray, Somali and Harari (about 30% of the population). Changed: every claim is now scoped to the surveyed areas, the national headcount estimate was removed, and the regional figure was relabelled as a 35-country average.

  2. 15 Oct 2026, independent AI review

    A separate Claude agent, given only the raw World Bank file and my claims (not my code), reproduced all eight key figures within 0.5 points, including the 1.69 design effect. It flagged "barely changes with income" as overclaimed, since each income group has wide intervals. Changed: the brief now says income shows no clear pattern and that a gap can't be ruled out, and the basic-phone figure (84%) is compared with phone owners who have an account (65%).
